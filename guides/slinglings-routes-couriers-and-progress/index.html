Slinglings Routes, Couriers and Progress: Unlocks and Play Order

By Nesim Razon, independent mobile developer

Slinglings: Cosmic Mail is a gravity sling puzzle with a clear structure: routes, levels, couriers and stamps. This guide does not teach aiming. It explains how the game is organized, what opens what, how stars work and a sensible order to play in.

How the campaign is built

The main campaign has 12 routes. Each route holds 20 numbered levels, and the 20th is called the Special Delivery, the big finale of that route. On top of that, every route hides one bonus postcard level, so there are 252 campaign levels in total.

There is a separate pool of Daily Letters as well. Everything together adds up to the 617 levels shipped inside the app: 240 route levels, 12 bonus postcards and 365 daily puzzles. They are stored in the game itself, so it all works offline, and every level was checked by a solver before it shipped, which means each one can be won.

The 12 routes and what each one adds

Every route introduces a new courier and a few new objects, so the game keeps changing instead of repeating one trick for 240 levels.

  • Route 1, Porch Orbit: planets and Pip, the classic courier.
  • Route 2, Bubble Wrap Bay: bouncy bumpers, and Zip.
  • Route 3, Night Mail: crates and lost-letter wells (the black holes), with Tubby.
  • Route 4, Sneezy Ridge: planets that push you away and express rings, with Puff.
  • Route 5, Tube Exchange: mail slots and pipes, with Duo.
  • Route 6, Clockwork Moons: orbiting and moving planets, with Plop.
  • Route 7, Fog Fields: ink clouds and fog, with Wisp.
  • Route 8, Gum Moons: sticky planets and walls, with Gum.
  • Route 9, Magnet Works: magnet strips and crates, with Clink.
  • Route 10, Switchyard: arrow gates, keys, locked doors and switches, with Rang.
  • Route 11, Crystal Quarry: crystals, pulsing planets and orbits, with Blink.
  • Route 12, Grand Sorting Hub: a mix of everything, played with all the couriers in turn.

When an object shows up for the first time, a short banner explains it once, so you never need to read a manual.

The 11 couriers

Each courier gets its own route, and it is the one you meet in that route's levels. The game picks the courier for you: you do not choose it before a level. Some couriers have an ability button that appears at the bottom of the screen during the flight; others are passive.

  • Pip (route 1): no ability, a plain flight.
  • Zip (route 2): BOOST gives an express burst of speed.
  • Tubby (route 3): heavy, shrugs off gravity and smashes crates. Passive.
  • Puff (route 4): FLOAT lets it hover for a moment.
  • Duo (route 5): SPLIT turns it into two couriers.
  • Plop (route 6): DROP stops it dead so it falls.
  • Wisp (route 7): passes through the first solid thing it meets. Passive.
  • Gum (route 8): sticks to green planets, then you shoot again from there.
  • Clink (route 9): magnet strips pull it, and FLIP reverses the pole.
  • Rang (route 10): REVERSE turns it around in mid-air.
  • Blink (route 11): BLINK jumps it ahead.

Levels are not locked to a single courier. Inside a route, a few levels in the middle and toward the end bring back the previous route's courier, so old skills stay fresh. The Courier Locker in the main menu shows every courier with its ability, and a courier opens as soon as its route does.

Stars, stamps and unlocking

Each level has up to three stars. You earn them by collecting stamps on the way to the mailbox. Reach the mailbox and you get one star; collect half of the stamps and you get two; collect all of them and you get three. Levels with only one or two stamps score by how many you grab. Level 8 of every route is an express delivery with five stamps: four of them give two stars, all five give three.

Levels open one after another: finish a level and the next one opens. A new route opens when you finish the last level of the previous route or when you hold 30 stars in it, whichever comes first. The bonus postcard of a route needs 36 stars in that route. Stars from a level you replay only ever go up, never down, so replaying is free of risk.

Hints and side goals

You start with 3 hints. A hint shows the exact shot that wins. More are earned by playing: you get one for every fifth new level you finish, one for every three side goals you collect, and you can watch a short optional video for 2 more. The side goals are small bonus challenges on a level: Fast, Close call and Clean (no bounces and no smashed crates).

A sensible order to play

Just follow the routes in order. The game is built so that each route teaches its own idea in the first levels, then mixes it with the basics, and finishes with that route's Special Delivery. Do not rush for three stars on the first pass; a one or two star clear is enough to move on, and you can come back to polish.

Difficulty follows a saw-tooth curve. Early levels in a route allow a wide margin of error, the margin narrows through the middle and the late levels, and the Special Delivery is a little more forgiving than the levels just before it. Each new route starts easy again. Later routes also ask for more precision overall, so if a stubborn level blocks you, play the bonus postcard or the Daily Letter and return fresh.

Daily Letter, badges and cloud save

The Daily Letter is one extra puzzle per day with a streak counter. Finishing the route levels also unlocks 12 accessories for the Locker (finish a route's last level to get its accessory) and 33 badges. You can sign in with Google Play Games for achievements, leaderboards and cloud save, and progress from two devices is merged so stars never go backwards. An optional small purchase removes ads for good.

Frequently asked questions

How many levels does Slinglings have?

There are 12 routes with 20 levels each, plus 12 bonus postcard levels. With 365 Daily Letters the app holds 617 levels in all.

How do I unlock a new route?

Finish the last level of the current route, or collect 30 stars in it. Either one opens the next route.

How do I open the bonus postcard?

Collect 36 stars in that route. The bonus then appears with a Bonus button on the level screen.

Can I choose my courier?

No. Each level uses a fixed courier. The Courier Locker shows all of them and their abilities, and a courier opens when its route does.

Do I need three stars everywhere?

No. Finishing a level is enough to move on. Stars matter for the bonus postcards, badges and bragging rights.
