How to Play Slinglings: Slingshot, Gravity, Stars and Hints

By Nesim Razon, independent mobile developer

Slinglings: Cosmic Mail is a physics puzzle about flinging a fluffy courier through space. You pull the sling back, let go, and the planets bend the flight toward a glowing mailbox. There is no timer and no lives, so you can experiment as much as you like. This guide covers the basics of aiming, how gravity behaves, how stars are earned and the common mistakes that cost the most shots.

The goal and your first shot

In every level you launch a courier so that it lands in the glowing mailbox ring. Touch anywhere on the screen, pull the character back toward the red ring, aim along the dotted line and let go. The dotted line is not a guess: it shows where the courier will fly, planets included. When the end of the line shows a green ring, the shot will deliver.

The pull distance sets the power, and the game shows it as a number out of 12 while you drag. The direction you pull is the opposite of the direction you fly, like a real sling. If that feels backwards at first, remember that you always pull away from the target.

How planets bend the flight

Every planet pulls the courier toward itself, and a bigger planet pulls harder. A flight is therefore a curve, not a straight line. Instead of aiming at the mailbox, aim at the space the planets will bend the shot through. Swinging around a planet is the basic trick of the whole game: you can reach a mailbox that sits behind an obstacle by letting a planet turn you around it.

Early routes only use planets. Later routes add new objects: black holes, bouncy bumpers, crates you can smash with speed, crystals, pipes, magnets, keys with locked doors, ink clouds and planets that move. When you meet something new, take one slow look at the level before shooting and ask what each object does to the flight.

Stamps, stars and side goals

Stamps float along the route. You collect them by flying through them, and your delivery is rated from one to three stars. Delivering always earns at least one star; collecting all the stamps in a level gives three, and collecting at least half gives two. On levels with only one or two stamps, each stamp adds a star. Some levels ask for the stamps in number order, and some have two mailboxes, where you must deliver to box 1 first and then box 2.

Three optional side goals are shown on the result card: Fast (a quick flight), Close call (grazing past something without hitting it) and Clean (no bumps and no smashed crates). They are remembered per level and add up over time. Every three side goals you earn also gives you one extra hint.

Couriers and their abilities

There are 11 couriers, and each route introduces a new one. Pip is the classic courier with no ability. Some couriers have a button that appears at the bottom of the screen while they fly: Zip has BOOST for an express burst, Puff has FLOAT to hover for a moment, Duo has SPLIT to become two couriers, Plop has DROP to stop dead and fall, Clink has FLIP to flip a magnetic pole, Rang has REVERSE to turn around and Blink has BLINK to jump ahead.

A few couriers have no button. Tubby is heavy, shrugs off gravity and smashes crates. Wisp passes through the first solid thing it meets. Gum sticks to green planets and you shoot again from there. While the courier is in flight, touching the rest of the screen does nothing, so the ability button is the only thing to press.

Hints, unlocking and routes

The lightbulb button helps when you are stuck. Its first press is free and shows only the angle: the shot flies along a gold beam, so you pull back the opposite way. A second press spends one of your hints and shows the full shot, including the power. Pull the character back to the red ring and the shot locks on. You start with 3 hints and can earn more by finishing new levels and hitting side goals. If you run out, a short optional video gives 2 more.

Finishing a level opens the next one. A new route opens when you finish the last level of the previous route or collect 30 stars on it. Collecting 36 stars on a route opens its bonus postcard level, and the last level of each route is a Special Delivery. Every level is checked by a solver, so there is always a way to win.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Aim in small steps. A one-degree change near a heavy planet can move the landing point a long way.
  • Use the dotted line first and fire second. Many failed shots are ones where the line never turned green.
  • Do not fight the pull. Instead of forcing a straight shot, find the planet that will curve the flight for you.
  • Watch moving planets. If a shot misses, wait and see where the planet will be when your courier arrives.
  • Do not press the fast-forward button hoping it will change the result. It only speeds up the replay and never changes where you land.
  • Read the line under the level title. It explains the courier's ability and any special rule, such as keys or stamp order.
  • Chase the third star after you can deliver, not before. A safe delivery first teaches you the level.

Aim modes and settings

If dragging with a fingertip feels fiddly, open the settings and try another aim mode. Classic is the standard pull. Slow Pull makes the aim move more gently, and Lock & fire locks the aim once you reach the ring so you can release at the right moment. Music, sounds and vibration can each be switched off. Slinglings plays offline and needs no account; the Daily Letter gives you a fresh level every day and keeps a streak.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a timer or a limited number of tries?

No. There is no timer and no lives, so you can fire as many shots as you like.

How many stars can I get on a level?

Three. You earn one for delivering, and more by collecting the stamps on the way.

What does the hint show?

The first press shows a free angle beam. A second press spends one hint and shows the whole shot.

Does fast-forward change the result?

No. It only speeds up the flight and never changes where the courier lands.

Can every level be won?

Yes. Every level is checked by a solver, so a winning shot always exists.
