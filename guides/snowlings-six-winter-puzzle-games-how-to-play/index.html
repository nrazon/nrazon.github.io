Snowlings: How to Play All 6 Winter Puzzle Games

By Nesim Razon, independent mobile developer

Snowlings hides six different puzzle games behind its daily doors. Each one is easy to learn and has a small trick that makes it click. This guide explains the goal, the controls and a few tips for every game, then shows how stars, hints and snowflakes work.

The six games at a glance

Every door in the calendar and every level in free play uses one of these games. Whichever you open, a one-line reminder of the goal appears at the top for your first three visits.

There is no timer in any of them. Take your time, restart whenever you like, and aim for the stars at the end.

  • Ice Slide: the penguin slides until something stops it; reach the gift.
  • Gift Sort: sort ornaments into tubes so each tube has one color.
  • Winter Mahjong: remove pairs of matching free tiles.
  • Snow Memory: flip cards and find the pairs.
  • Sled Run: send every sled off the board in a workable order.
  • Paint the Picture: use number clues to paint a hidden winter picture.

Ice Slide: slide to the gift

Swipe in any direction, or use the four arrow buttons under the board. The penguin does not walk one square; it keeps sliding until something stops it. A rock or snowman blocks the way, a snow pile stops you right on top of it, and the board edge stops you too. Slide onto the gift and you win.

Harder levels add two special tiles. Cracked ice can be crossed once: when you leave it, it turns into water and becomes a wall. Arrow tiles turn you in their direction as soon as you slide onto them.

  • Work backwards: look at the gift and ask which squares would let you stop next to it.
  • A snow pile is a handy brake because it stops you exactly where you want.
  • Before crossing cracked ice, check that you will not need that square again.

Stars depend on your number of slides. The shortest possible route earns three stars, up to two extra slides earns two, and anything longer earns one.

Gift Sort: one color per tube

Each tube holds four ornaments, and you always have two empty tubes to work with. Tap a tube to lift the group of same-colored ornaments on top, then tap where they should go. A move works if the target tube is empty, or if its top ornament has the same color and there is room. The whole matching group moves as far as space allows.

You win when every tube is either empty or full of a single color. Moving a tube that is already one color into an empty tube is not allowed, because it would not help.

  • Free an empty tube early; it is your most valuable tool.
  • Build on the colors that are already on top of a tube rather than burying them.
  • Use Undo freely. It appears as soon as you have made a move.

Three stars are given for finishing close to a good solution, so a few detours still count. Wasteful wandering lowers the score to two or one star.

Winter Mahjong: match free tiles

The pile has 72 tiles, 36 matching pairs of winter symbols. A tile is free when nothing lies on top of it and its left or right side is open. Tap one free tile, then another free tile with the same symbol, and the pair disappears. Tapping a tile that is not free just makes it shake.

Every deal is built so that it can be finished, and the free play section offers 30 different layouts. If you ever run out of moves, the remaining tiles are reshuffled for you.

  • Clear the tall stacks first so tiles underneath become free.
  • Before removing a pair, check whether a third tile with that symbol is buried; keep the pair that frees the most.
  • Picking two tiles that do not match counts as a mistake.

Two mistakes or fewer gives three stars, up to six gives two, and more than that gives one.

Snow Memory: find the pairs

Tap two cards. If the pictures match they stay face up. If not, they flip back after a short pause and that counts as a miss. Clear every pair to finish.

In free play you choose the size: small has 6 pairs, medium 8 and large 12. Layouts are random, so no two games are the same.

  • Start by flipping cards in a fixed order so you can remember what you saw.
  • A flipped card whose partner you already know is a free match, so use that knowledge first.
  • The hint button shows a matching pair for a moment.

Three stars need no more misses than half the number of pairs. Up to the number of pairs plus two gives two stars.

Sled Run: clear the track

Several sleds of different lengths sit on the board, each facing one direction. A sled can leave only when the path ahead of it, all the way to the edge, is empty. Tap a free sled and it slides off. Tap a blocked sled and it shakes and counts as a mistake. A counter shows how many sleds remain.

Taking a free sled away never blocks another sled, so the order among free sleds does not matter. What matters is which blocked sleds you unlock next.

  • Look at the sleds that already face an empty edge and send those first.
  • Trace the chain: sled A blocks B, B blocks C. Start at the end of the chain.
  • Longer sleds appear in the harder levels, so check their whole length.

With no mistakes you get three stars. Up to two mistakes gives two stars.

Paint the Picture: number puzzles

The numbers beside each row and above each column tell how many squares in a row are filled on that line. A clue of 3 1 means three filled squares, at least one gap, then one filled square. Tap a square to fill it and tap again to clear it. Switch to the cross mode to mark squares you know are empty, and drag your finger to fill several squares at once.

When a line matches its numbers, the numbers fade out. When every line matches, the winter picture appears. The first pictures are small 5 by 5 grids, and later ones are larger. A how-to panel opens during your first three visits, and the question mark button brings it back.

  • Start with lines whose numbers almost fill the whole width; they are decided at once.
  • Mark the sure empty squares with a cross so the picture stays clean.
  • Work line by line. Every picture can be reasoned out without guessing.

A hint fills or clears the first wrong square it finds. No hints gives three stars, one or two hints gives two stars, and more gives one.

Stars, hints and snowflakes

Each level gives one to three stars, and the app keeps your best result. Stars are earned in different ways in each game: slides in Ice Slide, moves in Gift Sort, mistakes in Winter Mahjong, Snow Memory and Sled Run, and hints in Paint the Picture.

You start with three free hints, and you earn one more for every three levels you solve, up to nine. When the hints run out, a short optional ad gives you one; if no ad is available, you still get the hint. In Ice Slide the hint points to the next slide, in Gift Sort it outlines two tubes, in Winter Mahjong it outlines a pair, and in Sled Run it outlines a free sled.

Each level you finish in free play also gives a snowflake, up to five a day. Three snowflakes open a missed door, so no door is ever lost.

Doors, free play and saving progress

In December the calendar rotates through the games, starting with Ice Slide on day one, then Gift Sort, Winter Mahjong, Snow Memory, Sled Run and Paint the Picture. Doors 24, 25 and 31 are picture puzzles. Free play has 60 Ice Slide levels, 100 Gift Sort levels, 30 Mahjong layouts, 60 Sled Run levels and 40 pictures, plus Snow Memory games that never run out. The first five levels of each game are open, and after that each level opens once you have solved the one before.

On Android, door and level stars are backed up with Google Play Games when you are signed in, so a new phone can pick up where the old one stopped. Snowflakes and hints stay on the device. Frequently asked questions

Is there a time limit in any Snowlings game?

No. None of the six games has a timer. Only moves, mistakes or hints affect your stars.

Can every level be solved?

Yes. The levels are generated or checked so that a solution always exists, including every Winter Mahjong deal.

What happens if I run out of hints?

You can watch a short optional ad to get one. If no ad is available at that moment, the hint is given anyway.

How many levels are there in free play?

There are 60 Ice Slide levels, 100 Gift Sort levels, 30 Mahjong layouts, 60 Sled Run levels and 40 Paint the Picture pictures, plus Snow Memory games without a fixed end.

Do I lose stars if I replay a level?

No. The app keeps your best star count for each level, so replaying can only improve it.
