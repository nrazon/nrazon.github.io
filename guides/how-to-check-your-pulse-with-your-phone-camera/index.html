How to Check Your Pulse With Your Phone Camera

By Nesim Razon, independent mobile developer

A phone can estimate your resting pulse with nothing but its rear camera and flash. It works well when your finger sits in the right place, and badly when it does not. This guide explains the idea in plain words, shows where to put your finger, what the hints on screen mean and how to read the result. Pulselings is not a medical device; it is a gentle way to keep an eye on your resting pulse.

How a camera can see a pulse

Each time your heart beats, a little more blood flows through the tip of your finger. When light from the flash shines through the fingertip, the amount of light that reaches the camera rises and falls very slightly with every beat. You cannot see this, but the camera can: the picture gets a tiny bit brighter and darker in step with your heart.

The app watches these small changes for a few seconds, filters out slow drifts and noise, counts the beats and turns the gaps between them into beats per minute. If the signal is too messy to count reliably, Pulselings says so instead of guessing a number.

Where to put your finger

The most common problem is the finger position, and it depends on your phone. On many phones the flash sits next to the lens, not on it. Rest the pad of your finger on the main back lens, then tilt the finger sideways and slide it toward the flash until it covers part of the flash as well. The light then passes through your finger and reaches the lens.

Rest the finger lightly. Pressing hard squeezes the blood out of the fingertip and weakens the signal. Cover the lens fully, so no outside light leaks in around the finger. Use the main back camera; the app stays on it on purpose.

While you measure

  • Sit down and rest your arm on a table. Moving your hand is the biggest cause of unclear results.
  • Breathe normally and do not talk.
  • A measurement usually takes about 20 to 40 seconds. If the signal is not clear yet, the app keeps going a little longer.
  • If the flash feels warm, lift your finger and try again later.
  • The screen stays on during the measurement, and there are no ads while you measure.

What the hints on screen mean

Pulselings shows short hints while it measures:

  • Place your fingertip over the main camera and the flash: no finger found yet.
  • The lens is covered but no light reaches it: slide your finger toward the flash.
  • The lens is not fully covered: rest your fingertip flat over it.
  • The light is very strong: rest your finger more lightly.
  • Weak signal: keep still and rest your arm on a table.
  • Light is low: slide your finger a little closer to the flash.

If your phone has no camera flash, the app warns you that measuring may not work well.

Reading the result

The result is shown as approximate and at rest. A camera cannot match a medical instrument, and the number depends on how still you were and how well the finger covered the lens. Measure sitting down, a few minutes after you have stopped moving, for numbers you can compare.

After the measurement you can tag it: at rest, after breathing, after exercise, after coffee or before sleep. The tag matters, because a pulse after a run and a pulse at the desk are different things.

Your own baseline

Pulselings does not compare you with other people. After five measurements tagged at rest within the last 14 days, it builds your personal baseline. From then on it tells you whether today looks rested, balanced or tired compared with your own resting average. Until then the app shows how many of the five resting measurements it has.

This is a gentle indication, not a medical result. A busy week, poor sleep or an extra coffee can all move your pulse.

Breathing mode and history

The breathing mode is a short exercise: breathe in for 4 seconds and out for 6 seconds, following a little animated heart, for 1, 2 or 3 minutes. Measuring again afterwards is a simple way to see how you feel.

The history screen keeps your measurements on your phone in a simple chart, with your average over the last 7 days. Press and hold an entry to delete it. There is no account, and your measurements are not sent anywhere.

What the app is not

Pulselings is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition, and it does not measure blood pressure or oxygen. A phone camera estimate can be wrong. If you feel unwell, or a result worries you, talk to a healthcare professional. If you feel faint, have chest pain or are short of breath, seek medical help instead of using an app.

Frequently asked questions

Which camera does Pulselings use?

The main rear camera, together with the flash. The app stays on this camera on purpose.

Why does it say the signal is not clear?

Usually because the finger moved, covers the lens only partly or is pressing too hard. Rest your arm on a table, cover the lens fully and rest the finger lightly.

How long does a measurement take?

About 20 to 40 seconds. If the signal is not clear yet, it measures a little longer.

How is my baseline calculated?

From your measurements tagged at rest in the last 14 days. You need five of them before the app compares today with your own average.

Is Pulselings a medical device?

No. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition. For medical advice, talk to a healthcare professional.
