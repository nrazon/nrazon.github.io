How to Solve Sliding Parking Puzzles: Free the Gold Pod

By Nesim Razon, independent Android developer

In a sliding parking puzzle you cannot lift or turn anything. You can only slide each piece along its own line. That sounds simple, yet the right order of moves can be hard to see. Here is a clear way to think about it.

The rules in one minute

The board is a square grid full of pods. Every pod is a horizontal or vertical strip of two, three or four squares, and it can only slide along its own axis: a horizontal pod moves left and right, a vertical pod moves up and down. Pods cannot pass through each other or through the walls.

One pod is gold. Your goal is to slide it out through the gate on the right edge of the board. In Nudgelings, which has this puzzle as its Slide mode, one move means dragging one pod as far as you like in one go. A long slide still counts as a single move, so where you stop matters.

Start at the exit, not at the gold pod

Beginners often push the gold pod forward until it hits something, then stare at the board. Do the opposite. Look at the squares between the gold pod and the gate. Each pod standing on that path is a blocker, and each blocker has to leave the row.

Now ask the same question for every blocker. A vertical blocker can only leave by moving up or down, so look at the squares above and below it. If those squares are taken, you have found the next blockers. Follow the chain until you reach a pod that can already move freely. That pod is where you begin.

Plan backwards

Backward planning means starting from the finished picture and asking what had to be true one move earlier. The last move is always the gold pod gliding to the gate, so the final position needs an empty path in its row. One move before that, the last blocker slid out of the way.

Work out which squares each blocker needs to be empty, and which pods are sitting on those squares. Write the chain in your head: to free A, move B; to move B, move C. Then play it in reverse order, starting from C. Most levels that feel impossible turn out to be a chain of only three or four links.

How to clear a jam

  • Count the free squares. Empty space is your only tool, so notice where it is and where it could be gathered in one place.
  • Look for a pod that is blocked on one side only. Moving it to the other side often opens a lane for two other pods at once.
  • Prefer a move that frees two things. A pod that unblocks only itself is usually a wasted move.
  • Do not park a pod in a spot that blocks the lane you will need later. When you slide a pod, check both ends of its range and choose the stop that stays out of the way.
  • If the same two pods keep taking turns, you are going in circles. Back up and try a different blocker.

Heavy pods, two gold pods and longer pods

Later worlds add new twists, and each one changes how you plan. Rocks are fixed blocks: they never move, so treat them like walls and plan around them.

A heavy pod, marked with an iron weight symbol, moves only one square per move. Because every square counts as a move, do not choose a heavy pod as your tool unless you have to, and leave it near where it will end up. Some levels have two gold pods and both must leave through their own gates, so you need to think about the order: freeing one pod can block the other. The long four-square pods fill an entire row or column, so they often act as a wall and must be dealt with early.

Stars, undo and hints

Each level gives up to three stars. You earn three stars by matching the shortest possible solution. A few extra moves still earn two stars, and simply finishing earns one. Do not chase the perfect score on the first try. Solve it first, then replay it and look for moves that did two jobs at once.

Undo is always free, so experiment. If you are stuck, you get 3 free hints a day, and an optional ad can give more. The app also gives a short note the first time a new kind of pod appears, which is a good moment to slow down and watch how it behaves.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the gold pod too early. It only needs to move at the very end, plus any time it blocks something.
  • Ignoring pods in the other direction. The pods that block you are often the vertical ones across the gold row.
  • Using many small moves when one long slide would do. Each drag is one move, so use the full range.
  • Giving up on a level after one failed plan. The first plan is usually the wrong chain, and restarting costs nothing.

A short routine for every level

Try the same routine each time and levels stop feeling random. First, find the gold pod and the gate. Second, list every pod that stands between them. Third, for each of those pods, check whether it can move now, and if not, which pod stops it. Fourth, find the end of the chain and play it backwards. Fifth, before each drag, look at the whole range the pod can slide and choose the stop that blocks nothing you will need. If a level still resists, take a breath and restart. A fresh start often shows a different chain within seconds, because you are no longer attached to your first idea.

Frequently asked questions

What is a sliding parking puzzle?

It is a grid puzzle where pieces can only slide along their own line, and you must free one special piece by moving the others out of the way.

How do I solve it faster?

Start from the exit, list the blockers in the path, then free each one in turn, working backwards from the last blocker.

Does a long slide count as one move in Nudgelings?

Yes. Dragging a pod any distance is one move, except heavy pods, which move only one square at a time.

Can I undo moves?

Yes. Undo is always free in the app, and you also get 3 free hints a day.

How do I get three stars?

Finish in as few moves as the shortest solution. Solve the level first, then replay it looking for moves that free two pods at once.
