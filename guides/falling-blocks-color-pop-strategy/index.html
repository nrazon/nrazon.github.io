Falling Blocks Color Pop: Rules, Chains and Strategy

By Nesim Razon, independent Android developer

In a color-pop falling-block game, pieces drop into a narrow well, but you do not clear rows. You clear colors. Connect ten or more squares of the same color and they pop, and what is above falls down and can pop again. Here are the rules and the habits that lead to long chains.

The rules in one minute

Pieces fall from the top of an 8-wide, 14-tall well. Each piece is one of the 12 pentominoes (five squares joined edge to edge), and each piece has a single color out of four. You slide it sideways, rotate it, and let it land.

When ten or more squares of the same color are connected edge to edge, they all pop together. The squares above fall down to fill the gap, and if that creates a new group of ten or more, it pops as well. That is a chain. There are no full-row clears in this mode: only color groups pop. The game ends when the stack reaches the top and a new piece has no room to appear.

Controls in Pentolings Drop

  • Slide your finger sideways to move the piece.
  • Tap the screen to rotate it.
  • Pull your finger down to make it fall faster. A quick flick down drops it at once.
  • A piece that touches the stack still moves for a moment (about half a second), so you can nudge or rotate it into place.
  • Next to the well you see the next two pieces, so you can plan one step ahead.

Strategy: build one big group of each color

  • Ten is a lot: it takes at least two pieces, and usually three. Do not scatter colors. Stack same-colored pieces side by side so they merge into one group.
  • Watch the small counter that appears on a group of 5 to 9 squares, such as 7/10. It shows how close the group is to popping, so you know which color needs one more piece.
  • Keep colors in separate zones if you can: red on the left, blue on the right. Mixed colors make groups that cannot merge.
  • When a piece does not match, put it on a flat spot where it will not split a growing group. A color that is almost ready should stay connected.

Strategy: set up chains

Points for a pop are the number of squares times 10, and the value is multiplied by the chain step. A second pop in the same chain is worth double, a third triple, and so on. So one planned chain is worth far more than several single pops.

To build a chain, prepare a second group that is nine squares or fewer in the layer that will fall. For example, keep a group of eight of color B just below a group of ten of color A. When A pops and the squares above come down, the pieces that fall may link into B and pop it too. Leave small gaps in mind: every square falls on its own, so the rest of a piece can drop after part of it pops.

Stay alive: speed, danger and backup

  • The well speeds up as you place more pieces, so use the early game to build a clean base. It speeds up every ten pieces, up to a fastest speed.
  • Keep the middle low. A frame appears when blocks reach the top three rows. That is the time to take a safe pop instead of chasing a chain.
  • Use the next-piece preview. If the following piece is a color you need, leave room for it.
  • In Pentolings you get one extra chance per run: after a game over you can watch an optional short ad and the top five rows are cleared so you can keep going.

A sample game plan

Start by building a flat base across the well. In the first ten pieces you only have a slow drop, so use the time to put same-colored pieces side by side and keep the surface even. Decide where each color lives, for example red on the left, blue in the middle and green on the right, and send matching pieces there.

When a group reaches about seven or eight squares, check the counter and look at the next-piece preview. If a matching piece is coming, wait for it and pop the group with a single placement. If not, put the piece somewhere that does not touch the almost-full group. Every time a pop opens a gap, look at what falls: a good move is one that lets a second group land next to its own color.

Common mistakes

  • Dropping with a flick too soon. A quick flick drops the piece at once and gives extra points, but you cannot nudge it afterwards. Use it only when you are sure of the spot.
  • Building a tall tower in the middle. The well is only 8 wide, so a high middle leaves the spawn area cramped and ends the game fast.
  • Mixing colors to fill holes. Small holes are tempting, but a piece of the wrong color splits a group into two groups that each need more pieces.
  • Ignoring the speed. The well speeds up every ten pieces, so a slow routine that worked early will not work later. Place pieces with fewer rotations.
  • Rotating at the last moment. A piece that touches the stack only waits a short moment before it locks, so rotate while it is still falling.

Frequently asked questions

How many squares make a group pop?

Ten or more squares of the same color connected edge to edge pop together.

Do rows clear in this game?

No. Rows never clear. Only same-color groups of ten or more disappear.

What is a chain?

When a pop makes the squares above fall and form a new group of ten or more, that second pop is a chain. Each step multiplies the points.

What does the 7/10 counter mean?

It shows a group of seven squares that needs ten to pop.

Is the daily Drop the same for everyone?

Yes. The sequence of pieces is the same for everyone on that day, and you can replay it.
