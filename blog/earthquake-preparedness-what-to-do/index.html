Earthquake Preparedness: Before, During and After (AFAD)

By Nesim Razon, independent Android developer · 2026-11-10

Earthquakes cannot be predicted, but a few simple preparations make a real difference. This article summarizes the public guidance of AFAD, Turkey's Disaster and Emergency Management Presidency, in plain language. It is a general summary, not a replacement for official instructions: for the current and complete guidance, visit afad.gov.tr.

Before: know your risk and make a plan

AFAD recommends learning the earthquake hazard of the place where your building stands; it provides a hazard map service for this purpose at tdth.afad.gov.tr. It also recommends that every household make a family plan and practice it, so that everyone knows what to do without having to think.

Part of the plan is a meeting place for the family after an earthquake, and knowing your nearest emergency gathering area. AFAD says you can look up the gathering area closest to your address through e-Devlet.

Before: secure your home

Heavy furniture can fall and injure people. AFAD's guidance includes fixing items such as wardrobes, bookcases, the refrigerator and the water heater to the wall or floor so they cannot topple during shaking. Keep exits and corridors free, and think about where the safest spot in each room is, such as next to a sturdy piece of furniture and away from windows and unfixed shelves.

Before: the disaster and emergency bag

AFAD recommends a disaster and emergency bag that can cover your basic needs for roughly the first 72 hours, until help reaches you. Keep it where you can reach it quickly, and check it from time to time so food, water and batteries are not out of date. Typical items in AFAD's list are:

  • Water, with the amount chosen by the number of people, and long-lasting, high-energy food such as canned food or dried fruit
  • A first aid kit and the personal medicines each person needs
  • A battery-powered radio, a flashlight and spare batteries
  • A whistle, so rescuers can find you
  • Copies of important documents, a list of phone numbers and, in some lists, some cash
  • Clean clothes, a raincoat, a blanket or sleeping bag, hygiene items, a face mask and a notebook with a pen
  • Items for babies, older people, people with disabilities and pets, if they are part of your household

During: Drop, Cover and Hold On

AFAD teaches the Drop-Cover-Hold On movement (Çök-Kapan-Tutun in Turkish). Get down on your knees (drop), protect your head and neck (cover), and hold on to something stable so you are not thrown around (hold on). A sturdy table or the side of strong furniture is preferred, away from windows and unfixed objects.

  • Do not run to the stairs or the exits while the ground is shaking; stay inside until the shaking stops, then leave.
  • Do not go out on the balcony and do not jump from a window or balcony.
  • Do not use the elevator.
  • If you are outdoors, move away from power lines, poles, trees and buildings and wall bases.
  • If you are driving, AFAD advises going to an open area once the shaking has stopped.

After: first steps

When the shaking stops, look around and check your own safety before leaving the building. AFAD's guidance then includes turning off the gas, water and electricity valves, taking your disaster bag, and going to the gathering area.

Aftershocks are normal after a large earthquake, so do not enter damaged buildings until it is clearly safe to do so. Istanbul's AFAD directorate also asks people to avoid using cars in the first six hours so emergency vehicles can pass freely, and to prefer SMS or internet messaging over voice calls so the phone network is free for emergencies.

After: if someone is trapped

AFAD's guidance for people trapped under rubble is to stay calm, not to light a match or lighter, to avoid stirring up dust, and to let rescuers know where you are by tapping on a pipe or a wall or by using a whistle. Shouting is advised as a last resort because it makes you inhale dust. In an emergency, call 112.

A short checklist

  • Check your area's earthquake hazard and your nearest gathering area.
  • Agree on a family meeting place and practice Drop-Cover-Hold On.
  • Fix heavy furniture and appliances to the wall or floor.
  • Prepare one bag per person, keep it within reach and refresh it regularly.
  • During shaking: drop, cover, hold on; no elevator, no balcony.
  • Afterwards: check around, close gas, water and electricity, take your bag, go to the gathering area.
  • Use messaging instead of calls, leave the roads free, and call 112 for emergencies.
  • Follow official information from AFAD, not social media rumors.

Practice with your family

A plan only works if everyone knows it. AFAD asks households to rehearse the family plan, including the Drop-Cover-Hold On movement. Children learn it quickly when it is treated like a short game: pick a sturdy table in each room, and try the movement together a couple of times a year. Also agree on how family members will find each other if you are apart, such as the meeting place you chose, and keep the phone numbers of relatives written on paper in the bag in case a phone battery runs out.

Information after an earthquake

After a strong earthquake, rumors spread quickly. Rely on the announcements of AFAD and the local authorities for the real situation, for example which areas are safe and where the gathering areas are. Keep your phone battery for important calls and messages, and keep the battery-powered radio from the bag handy, since mobile networks can be busy.

If you are not in the affected area, avoid calling people who may be there, so the lines stay free for those who need emergency help.

Frequently asked questions

What is Drop-Cover-Hold On?

It is the movement AFAD teaches for shaking: drop to your knees, cover your head and neck, and hold on to something stable.

How long should a disaster bag last?

It is meant to cover your basic needs for roughly the first 72 hours, until help arrives.

Should I run outside during the shaking?

No. AFAD advises not to run to the stairs or exits during shaking, to stay inside until it stops and then leave carefully.

Which number do I call in an emergency?

112. After an earthquake, prefer SMS or internet messaging for non-urgent contact to keep the phone network free.

Where can I find the official guidance?

On the AFAD website, afad.gov.tr, and on the pages of the provincial AFAD directorates.
