New Year's Eve Games at Home for the Whole Family

By Nesim Razon, independent mobile developer · 2026-11-28

#Game design

A New Year's Eve at home has a long middle: dinner is over and midnight is still far away. Games fill that gap well when everyone can join, from a young child to a grandparent. Here are games that need little more than paper, pencils and a few small things from the kitchen, plus a plan for fitting them into one evening.

Choose games for the people, not the other way round

Before picking anything, look at who will be at the table. The youngest player sets the limit for reading and writing. The oldest may prefer to sit and may not enjoy shouting across the room. A guest who has never met the family needs a game with no inside jokes.

Good evening games share three things. The rules take about a minute to explain. People can join late or leave for the kitchen without breaking the round. Losing is quick and painless, so nobody sits out for half an hour. If a game fails one of these, keep it for another day.

Tombala, the classic of the evening

Tombala is the Turkish family version of a number game played with the numbers one to ninety. Each card has fifteen numbers in three rows. One person draws numbers and calls them out, and players mark the ones on their cards.

Prizes come in order: first the player who completes one row, then the one who completes two rows, and finally the full card. Use things nobody can be upset about losing: a chocolate, choosing the next song, being excused from washing up. Keep money out of it, especially with children at the table.

Let a different person call numbers each round. Calling is the best seat in the house for someone who does not want to keep track of cards.

A gift draw so everyone receives something

If buying a present for every person is too much, draw names so each person buys one gift for one other person. Agree on a modest limit and on what to do for the youngest children.

Paper slips in a bowl work, as long as someone checks that nobody drew their own name. When relatives are not in the same room, there is a free draw tool on this site at nesimapps.com/secret-santa, with no sign-up. Do the draw some days ahead, because the fun of midnight is opening, not shopping.

Word games with no equipment

  • Word chain: each person says a word that starts with the last letter of the previous word. Pick a theme, such as food or winter, to raise the difficulty.
  • Twenty questions: one player thinks of a thing and the others have twenty yes or no questions to find it.
  • The suitcase game: the first player says what they pack for the new year, the next repeats it and adds one item, and the chain grows until someone forgets.
  • One word story: the family builds a story, one word each. It goes strange very quickly, which is the point.

Games that get people moving

After dinner, something on foot helps. Acting without words needs only a bowl of slips with actions or animals written on them. A player takes a slip and acts it out while their team guesses.

For drawing and guessing, one person draws while the others shout answers. A paper pad is enough, and bad drawings are more entertaining than good ones.

Guess who I am works with sticky notes: each person has a name or an animal on their forehead and asks yes or no questions until they work it out. Choose subjects that every age knows, such as animals, foods and family members.

A quiet table for the late hours

Some people get tired before midnight. Set up a corner with something calm: a deck of cards, a jigsaw, colouring pages or a memory game made from pairs of paper squares.

This is also a good moment for a wish jar. Everyone writes one wish or one good memory of the year on a slip. Fold them, put them in a jar and read them after midnight, or save the jar to open next December. It takes five minutes and is often the part people remember.

A plan from dinner to midnight

A loose order keeps the evening from collapsing into screens. Right after dinner, start with something that gets people standing, such as acting without words. When the room settles, play two or three rounds of tombala with tea and snacks. Then offer the quiet corner while others continue with word games.

Stop all games about ten minutes before midnight. Find glasses, call in whoever is in the kitchen and check one clock together, so the countdown is shared. After midnight, one short round of whatever was most popular is a good way to end without everyone leaving the table at once.

Keeping it kind

  • Mix teams by age, so no team is all children or all adults.
  • Let small children pair with an adult instead of playing alone.
  • Skip questions about weight, money, marriage or school results.
  • Allow anyone to watch without explaining why.
  • End a game while people still enjoy it.

What to prepare the day before

Ten minutes of preparation saves the evening from searching drawers at nine o'clock. Put pencils and a pad of paper in one box. Cut slips for acting and for the name games and write them in advance, mixing easy and harder ones. Check that you have enough number cards for tombala and something small to mark them with, such as dried beans or buttons, keeping those away from very young children.

Decide who will be in charge of the first game. A host who has to cook, serve and explain rules at once usually ends up skipping the games altogether, so hand one of them to a relative or an older child.

Pencil and paper favourites

The categories game needs only a sheet each. Draw columns for names, places, animals and things. One player picks a letter, everyone fills the columns with words starting with it, and the first to finish calls stop. Unique answers score more than repeated ones, which rewards imagination.

A noughts and crosses tournament is another easy filler: pair people at random, play best of three and let the winners meet. It is over quickly, which makes it good for the minutes while the dessert is being served.

When part of the family is far away

A video call can carry more than greetings. Tombala works if each home has its own cards and one person calls the numbers on camera. Word chain and twenty questions need no changes at all. Choose one device with the volume up so that the whole room hears, and keep the remote session short, since delay makes shouting games frustrating.

Agree on the time in advance, especially across time zones, so that nobody is called while still cooking or already asleep.

Snacks, breaks and small children

Games go better with food nearby that does not leave sticky fingers on the cards. Plan a break between the lively part and the long round of tombala, and open a window. Young children often manage one or two games before they need to move; let them leave and come back without making it an event.

Frequently asked questions

What games can we play on New Year's Eve without buying anything?

Word chain, twenty questions, the suitcase game, acting without words and drawing and guessing need only paper and pencils or nothing at all.

How do you play tombala?

Numbers from one to ninety are drawn and called. Players mark their cards, and prizes go to one row, two rows and then the full card.

Which games suit both children and grandparents?

Tombala, a wish jar and guess who I am work well, because the rules are short and teams can mix ages.

How long should each game last?

Keep rounds short, around ten to fifteen minutes, and stop while people still want more.
