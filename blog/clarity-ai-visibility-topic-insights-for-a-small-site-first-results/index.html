Clarity AI Visibility: Topic Insights for a Small Site, First Results

By Nesim Razon, independent mobile developer · 2026-11-21

#Dev notes

Microsoft Clarity has an AI Visibility section with Topic insights. You give it a topic and ten to fifteen questions, it asks AI assistants those questions and measures how often your pages are cited. I set it up for my small developer site and read the first report. Here is how it works, what it showed and the caution I would put next to every number.

What you set up

A topic needs a title and between ten and fifteen prompts, which are questions your audience might ask an AI assistant. There is a weekly limit on reports. After you press the button the report takes a few minutes to generate, and Clarity shows a progress percentage while it works. I chose one topic about small puzzle games and cozy apps and wrote fifteen questions based on the articles I already have.

What the report measures

  • Citation rate: in how many of the questions your pages are cited.
  • Share of authority: your share of all cited pages compared with other domains.
  • Answer contribution: how much of an answer that cites you comes from your pages.
  • Content opportunities: subjects where other sources are cited and you are not.
  • Your top pages: which of your pages were used and for what.

What it found for me

My pages were cited in about thirteen percent of the questions, which is roughly two out of fifteen. I had ten citations, around nine percent of all citations, in the same range as GitHub and Product Hunt and ahead of Google itself. When my pages were cited, about half of the answer came from them.

The two cited articles were both technical: how to merge cloud save progress between two platforms, and how to debug an iPhone from Windows over USB. None of my game pages or guides were cited.

Where the report sees opportunities

Three areas were marked as gaps: choosing a cozy puzzle game, launching a game as a solo developer and explaining the mechanics of puzzles. In each of them other sites are cited and mine are not. I treat them as a to-do list: a guide on how to choose a cozy puzzle game, a launch write-up with real numbers and clearer answers in my puzzle articles.

The caution

I wrote the questions from my own articles, so the citation rate is flattered. A real audience asks different questions. The sample is also small and the report is marked as beta. I read the first report as a starting point and I will run it again after I change something, so that I can compare before and after instead of trusting a single number.

What I changed because of it

  • Wrote a guide on choosing a cozy puzzle game for Android.
  • Made a list of articles where the first two sentences should answer the question directly.
  • Planned a launch article with real numbers after the launch is over.
  • Scheduled the next report run to compare with this one.

Frequently asked questions

What is Clarity Topic insights?

A beta tool in Microsoft Clarity that asks AI assistants your chosen questions and reports how often your pages are cited and by what share.

How many questions can I add?

Between ten and fifteen prompts per topic.

Can I trust the citation rate?

Use it as a starting point. It depends on the questions you wrote, the sample is small and the feature is in beta.

How do I improve my AI visibility?

Answer questions directly near the top of pages, cover the gaps the report lists and compare reports over time.
